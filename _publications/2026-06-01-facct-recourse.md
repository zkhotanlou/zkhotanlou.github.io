Studied algorithmic recourse in multi-agent settings, where recommendations given to one individual can affect the outcomes available to others. Formulated the problem as a capacity-constrained matching problem and developed an MILP optimization framework for allocating recourse recommendations across multiple individuals, achieving 97–99% of achievable social welfare with limited changes to the system.

Also presented at the NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on Algorithmic Collective Action, and at Remarkable 2026, hosted by the Vector Institute.
